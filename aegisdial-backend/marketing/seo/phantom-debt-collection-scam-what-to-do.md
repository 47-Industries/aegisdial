# Phantom debt collector scam — what to do

A phantom debt collector scam leans on threats plus personal details from old breaches to make a debt feel real even when it isn't. If a caller threatened arrest, garnishment, or a lawsuit over a debt you don't recognize, you have rights — and you do not pay today.

## What happens in this scam

The caller demands payment on an old payday loan, credit card, or utility bill you don't recognize — or one you paid off years ago. They cite your SSN, old address, or employer to prove it's "real," and threaten wage garnishment, arrest, or a lawsuit by end of day. They push for payment today by gift card, prepaid card, or wire, bypassing the normal written-notice requirements.

## Why it worked on you (it's not your fault)

Fear of a lawsuit plus authentic-sounding personal details (from old data breaches) make the debt feel legitimate even when it isn't. Many people have had past debt — forgotten medical bills, student loans, old utilities — and the caller weaponizes that ambiguity. This works on financially organized, careful adults. Routinely.

## What to do in the next 15 minutes

1. Do not pay on this call. Federal law (the FDCPA) gives you the right to a written debt validation letter before you owe anything.
2. Ask for the letter. Demand it in writing. Phantom collectors cannot produce it; real collectors must.
3. Get the caller's business name, agent name, and collector license number. Write them down. If they refuse, that alone is illegal under the FDCPA.
4. Do not give your SSN, full bank account, or card info on this call.
5. Pull your free credit reports at annualcreditreport.com. If the debt is real, it will show up. If it doesn't, the caller is a scammer.
6. Place a fraud alert at Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion if your SSN or account was quoted.
7. File with the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau at consumerfinance.gov/complaint and your state attorney general.
8. File at reportfraud.ftc.gov.

## Red flags you might have missed (for next time)

- No written validation notice before the call — illegal under the FDCPA.
- Threats of arrest, wage garnishment, or deportation for a debt.
- Demanded payment by gift card, prepaid card, or wire.
- Refused to name the original creditor or provide their collector license.
- Called repeatedly outside 8 a.m. to 9 p.m. or at your workplace after you said stop.
