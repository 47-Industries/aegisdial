# Grandchild emergency scam — what to do if you sent money

If the grandchild emergency scam just hit you, you didn't fall for anything — you responded exactly the way a loving grandparent is supposed to respond. The scammers know that, and they built the script around it. Here is what to do in the next hour, in plain order.

## What happens in this scam

The call opens with a crying voice — "Grandma? It's me, I'm in trouble" — and waits for you to say the grandchild's name. A second voice jumps in as a lawyer, bail bondsman, or officer, claiming your grandchild was arrested after an accident, usually with a "gag order" so you can't tell other family. Then a courier is dispatched to your house to pick up cash, gift cards, or a cashier's check for bail.

## Why it worked on you (it's not your fault)

Love, panic, and protective instinct override every other signal. The manufactured "gag order" is engineered specifically to keep you from the one person who could stop this — another family member who would recognize the ruse in two seconds. That isolation tactic is used by professional fraud crews precisely because it works on thoughtful people, not careless ones.

## What to do in the next 15 minutes

1. Call your grandchild directly, then call their parents. Do not wait.
2. If a courier has been dispatched and hasn't arrived yet, call your local police non-emergency line and tell them. In many cities, police have successfully intercepted couriers mid-run.
3. If you already handed over cash, save everything — photos of the courier, car, plate, any receipt or envelope — and call the non-emergency police line to file a report.
4. If you bought gift cards, call each issuer's fraud line on the packaging. Some balances are recoverable if codes haven't been redeemed.
5. If you sent a wire or cashier's check, call your bank now on the card number and ask for a recall. Timing window is hours, not days.
6. File at reportfraud.ftc.gov and at ic3.gov.
7. Agree on a family safe word today. One word only you and your family know.

## Red flags you might have missed (for next time)

- Caller wouldn't give a name until you guessed it.
- Told you not to tell their parents or other family.
- Sent a courier to pick up cash or gift cards.
- Claimed a "gag order" or "court-sealed case."
- Story involved a pregnant woman, a DUI, or an out-of-state accident — all common scripts.
