# Car warranty expiration scam — what to do

A car warranty expiration scam is one of the most common robocalls still running. If you signed up after one of those calls and now can't get a straight answer from the "warranty center," here's how to dispute and get clean.

## What happens in this scam

A robocall or live caller says your vehicle's manufacturer warranty is about to expire and they're calling from "the warranty center" for your specific make and model. A "specialist" collects your VIN, mileage, and card info to enroll you in extended coverage for hundreds or thousands of dollars. Either nothing is delivered, or a worthless third-party service contract is issued that denies nearly every claim.

## Why it worked on you (it's not your fault)

Most drivers don't know whether their manufacturer warranty is active, and the caller sounds specific enough — your exact make, model, and mileage — to be plausible. That specificity usually comes from data broker lists, not from the manufacturer. Millions of these calls go out every week, and the scripts are refined constantly.

## What to do in the next 15 minutes

1. If you paid by credit card in the last 60 days, dispute the charge with your card issuer as "services not as described." Regulation Z gives you that right.
2. Stop autopay. Remove the card from any portal the "warranty center" gave you, and ask your card issuer to block that merchant.
3. If you paid by debit or ACH, call your bank on the number from your card and open a dispute.
4. If you signed a contract, look for a cancellation window — many state laws require a cooling-off period (often 30 days) for vehicle service contracts.
5. Check your actual manufacturer warranty status by contacting your vehicle's OEM (Ford, GM, Toyota, Honda, Stellantis, Tesla) using the number in your owner's manual or on their official site.
6. File with your state attorney general consumer protection and with the FTC at reportfraud.ftc.gov.
7. If large amounts were charged, also file at ic3.gov.
8. Save every document, robocall voicemail, and receipt — these help disputes succeed.

## Red flags you might have missed (for next time)

- Robocall or cold call about your specific car "warranty expiring."
- Asked for VIN, mileage, and card info on the same call.
- Wouldn't name the underwriter in writing before charging you.
- High-pressure "today only" pricing.
- The caller can't tell you anything about your vehicle they couldn't have bought from a data broker.
