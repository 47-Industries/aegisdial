# Sextortion scam — they threatened to leak photos

A sextortion scam is engineered to make you feel alone and rushed, because that's the only state in which paying feels like a solution. It isn't. Paying almost always leads to more demands. You are going to be okay. Here's the plan.

## What happens in this scam

A flirty chat online — often through a dating app or DMs — moves quickly to exchanging explicit images. Then the scammer reveals they have the images (and often a contact list scraped from social media) and demands payment within hours or they'll send the images to your family, coworkers, or followers. They collect by Cash App, PayPal, gift cards, or crypto — and frequently come back for more after the first payment.

## Why it worked on you (it's not your fault)

Shame and panic collapse the normal 24-hour pause. You try to handle it alone, which is exactly what the extortionist needs. Sextortion targets everyone — teens, adult men, professional women, grandparents on a dating app. The crews running this are organized and run the play thousands of times a day.

## What to do in the next 15 minutes

1. Do not pay. Paying does not end it; it accelerates the demands.
2. Stop responding to the account. Do not delete the messages — screenshot everything first.
3. Lock down privacy on every social account: set followers to private, remove your contact list from public view, and block the account from finding you. Many platforms have emergency "account takeover / extortion" flows in their help center.
4. Report the account on the platform where the extortion happened (Instagram, Snapchat, Facebook, Discord, dating app).
5. If the target is a minor, call the NCMEC CyberTipline at cybertipline.org (or 1-800-843-5678). Tell a trusted adult.
6. File at reportfraud.ftc.gov and at ic3.gov.
7. If payment already went out, call your bank on the number from your card or dispute via the app. Save transaction IDs.
8. Tell one trusted friend, family member, or therapist. Shame is the weapon. Saying it out loud defuses it.

## Red flags you might have missed (for next time)

- New online contact who moved very fast to sexual content.
- Threat to share images with a specific list of your real contacts.
- Demanded payment in crypto, Cash App, or gift cards, "or in one hour I post."
- Came back for a second payment after you paid the first.
- Account had very few posts, stolen-looking photos, or was created recently.
