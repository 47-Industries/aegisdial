# SIM swap attack — what to do if your phone is dead

If your phone suddenly shows "No service" or "SOS only" and your bank just emailed about a login you didn't do, you may be in a SIM swap attack right now. The window is minutes, not hours. Here's the exact order.

## What happens in this scam

The attacker collects enough of your info from breaches and social engineering to impersonate you to your carrier. They call the carrier claiming a lost phone and request a SIM swap or port-out to a new carrier. Once your number is on their SIM, they intercept banking 2FA SMS codes and reset passwords at your bank, brokerage, and crypto exchange within minutes.

## Why it worked on you (it's not your fault)

Carriers have historically trained their reps to resolve customer frustration quickly, which is exactly the attack surface SIM swappers exploit. Account PINs are often weak or reused. The victim sees service drop and doesn't immediately realize why — by the time you check, money may already have moved.

## What to do in the next 15 minutes

1. Use another phone (spouse, friend, a landline) to call your carrier right now. Say "my number has been ported or SIM-swapped without my consent, please reverse it and put a port-out PIN on the account." Stay on the line.
2. From a trusted device (a laptop or iPad still logged in), change your email password first — email resets are the master key.
3. Change passwords at your bank, brokerage, crypto exchange, and any financial account. Pull 2FA off SMS onto an authenticator app (Authy, Google Authenticator, 1Password).
4. Call your bank and brokerage on the numbers on the back of your card. Tell them a SIM swap is in progress and to freeze outbound wires and ACH.
5. Log out of all sessions on Google and Apple ID from your trusted device.
6. Lock your credit at Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion.
7. File at reportfraud.ftc.gov and at ic3.gov if money moved.
8. Ask your carrier for a port-out PIN and a number-lock going forward.

## Red flags you might have missed (for next time)

- Your phone suddenly shows "No service" or "SOS only" for no weather or outage reason.
- You got a text or email confirming a SIM change or number transfer you didn't request.
- You stopped receiving 2FA codes but your Wi-Fi still works.
- Your bank emailed about a login, transfer, or password change you didn't make.
- Family members say they got a weird text from "you" asking for money or codes.
