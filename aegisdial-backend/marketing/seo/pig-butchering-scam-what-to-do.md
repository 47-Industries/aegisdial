# Pig butchering / crypto investment scam — what to do now

If a pig butchering crypto investment scam has you staring at a withdrawal screen that won't release your money, there is no "tax" or "unlock fee" that will work. There never was. Stop paying and read this — the next 24 hours are where you protect the rest.

## What happens in this scam

A "wrong number" text or a dating-app match turns into a warm, daily conversation over weeks or months. The new friend casually mentions their uncle's crypto or forex tips, walks you through a small deposit on a polished-looking platform, and lets you withdraw a small profit to build trust. Then you get coached into bigger deposits — often retirement or home equity — and when you try to withdraw, the platform demands "taxes" or "release fees" that only grow.

## Why it worked on you (it's not your fault)

This scam stacks the entire social-engineering toolkit on a long timeline: reciprocity, sunk cost, loneliness, FOMO, and a staged early win that feels like proof. By the time the biggest deposits were made, you genuinely believed you had a partner and a strategy. The crews running this are industrial and well-funded — they do this to thousands of people in parallel.

## What to do in the next 24 hours

1. Stop all further deposits today. "Tax to withdraw" is not a real step in any real platform; paying it never works.
2. Do not respond to the "friend" anymore. They will escalate with new stories or threats. Mute them.
3. Screenshot everything: chats, the platform UI, deposit receipts, wallet addresses, and transaction IDs. Save it to a separate folder.
4. File at ic3.gov (FBI) with all transaction IDs and wallet addresses. This is the single most important filing — ic3 aggregates crypto traces across cases.
5. File at reportfraud.ftc.gov and with your state securities regulator and state AG.
6. If any wire from your bank went to the platform, call your bank on the card's number immediately about a recall.
7. Change passwords on email, bank, brokerage, and any exchange you used. Pull 2FA off SMS onto an authenticator app.
8. Tell one trusted family member. Recovery-scam callers will come next, claiming they can get the crypto back for a fee — they cannot, and they know who you are.

## Red flags you might have missed (for next time)

- A stranger's "wrong number" text led to a months-long relationship you never met in person.
- They mentioned a mentor, uncle, or insider with special trading signals.
- The platform let you withdraw small amounts early — classic confidence-building.
- Withdrawal suddenly required "taxes," "liquidity fees," or a deposit to "unlock."
- You were coached to move retirement, home equity, or borrowed money into the platform.
