# Tech support scam — what to do if you gave them remote access

If a tech support scam got you to install AnyDesk, TeamViewer, or UltraViewer, treat the next 15 minutes like a fire. The attacker may still be on your machine, and the window to protect your bank, email, and identity is open right now.

## What happens in this scam

A pop-up, call, or email warns that your computer is infected, your Apple ID is compromised, or your router has been "hacked from Russia." The "technician" walks you into installing remote-access software so they can "run a scan." Once they have your screen, they open your banking, stage fake "hacker transfers" to alarm you, and talk you into moving real money to a "safe" account or buying gift cards to "trap" the hacker.

## Why it worked on you (it's not your fault)

You are not a computer security expert. They are. Once they are on your screen, the authority gap is total — everything they show you looks real because they are literally drawing it for you. Professional fraud crews use this playbook because it works on college professors, CPAs, and engineers. Not because you were careless.

## What to do in the next 15 minutes

1. Disconnect your computer from Wi-Fi and unplug any ethernet cable. Right now.
2. From a different device (phone, another laptop), change your email password and your bank password. Pull 2FA off SMS onto an authenticator app.
3. Call your bank on the number printed on the back of your card. Tell them you gave someone remote access. Any wires or Zelle sent in the last hour may still be stoppable.
4. Uninstall AnyDesk, TeamViewer, UltraViewer, or any remote-access app they had you install. If you can't tell what they installed, treat the machine as compromised and plan a full wipe or a trusted repair.
5. Review recent logins on your Google or Apple account from a trusted device and sign out of unknown sessions.
6. Lock your credit at all three bureaus if they saw your SSN or ID on screen.
7. File at reportfraud.ftc.gov and at ic3.gov if money moved.

## Red flags you might have missed (for next time)

- A pop-up or call asked you to install remote-access software.
- Showed you "hacker activity" on your screen you'd never seen before.
- Asked you to log into your bank while they watched.
- Directed you to gift cards or wire transfers to "trap" or "trace" the hacker.
- Would not let you hang up and restart the call from your bank's official number.
