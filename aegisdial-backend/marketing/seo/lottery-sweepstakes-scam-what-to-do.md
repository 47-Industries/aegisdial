# Lottery or sweepstakes scam — 'you won' but you pay first

A lottery or sweepstakes scam tells you you've won big — Publishers Clearing House, a state lottery, an international drawing — and then asks you to pay fees before the prize is released. Real prizes never work that way. If you've already sent money, here is what to do.

## What happens in this scam

You get a call, letter, or Facebook message saying you've won Publishers Clearing House, a state lottery, or an international sweepstakes. They say you must prepay taxes, IRS fees, customs, or insurance to release the prize — often by gift card, wire, or check (the check bounces later). Either nothing arrives, or a follow-up demands another fee "because a second prize was added."

## Why it worked on you (it's not your fault)

The excitement of winning plus confusion about tax rules (you might actually owe taxes on real winnings) makes the request feel normal. This scam specifically targets older adults with careful, trusting personalities, and the scripts are tuned for emotional warmth. Falling for one is not a character flaw. It's a scam that was made for exactly this moment.

## What to do in the next 15 minutes

1. Do not send another dollar, card number, or gift card code.
2. If you wired money, call your bank on the number from your card. Recalls are possible within narrow windows.
3. If you gift-carded, call each issuer's fraud line on the packaging before the codes are fully redeemed.
4. If you deposited a check they sent you and forwarded part of the money, call your bank immediately — the check will bounce in days and leave you liable for the full amount.
5. Save everything: letters, envelopes, emails, chat logs, phone numbers, names used. These help filings.
6. If you think you entered a real sweepstakes, verify only through the sponsor's official website — never through a number or link in their message.
7. File at reportfraud.ftc.gov. If international wires went out, also file at ic3.gov.
8. Tell one family member. Scam lists resell repeatedly — follow-up "recovery" scams will come next.

## Red flags you might have missed (for next time)

- You never entered the lottery or sweepstakes you "won."
- Required to pay fees up front — real prizes deduct taxes, they don't collect them by gift card.
- Told to keep the win confidential "until funds clear."
- PCH reps showing up at your door — real PCH only does this for the big ones and never asks for payment.
- Caller used a free email or a foreign phone number.
