# Digital arrest scam — fake police wanting to jail you

A digital arrest scam is one of the most coercive scripts running today. If you were on a video call for hours with someone calling themselves police, customs, or a federal agent — "digital arrest" is not a real legal concept anywhere in the US. You are not under arrest. Walk away from the phone.

## What happens in this scam

The caller claims to be police, customs, or a federal agency saying your identity has been used in a serious crime and you are now under "digital house arrest" — you must stay on the phone and on camera at home until cleared. They keep you on a video or audio line for hours, sometimes days, forbidding you from telling anyone. Then they demand you transfer all "suspect" funds to a government-verified account for "auditing," and the money disappears.

## Why it worked on you (it's not your fault)

Sustained isolation plus fear of arrest is a textbook coercion technique used by hostage negotiators — in this case against you. Hours on the call with no breaks wears down anyone's ability to reason or seek help. This scam has been run against judges, doctors, and executives. Falling under its pressure says something about the method, not you.

## What to do in the next 15 minutes

1. Hang up and walk away from the phone. Turn off the camera. Close the laptop. The isolation is the scam — breaking it is the first real move.
2. Tell a family member or neighbor in person or by call from another phone. Say out loud: "Someone was pretending to be police."
3. Call your local police non-emergency line using the number from their official website. Report what happened.
4. If money moved, call your bank on the number from your card immediately. Wire and Zelle recalls are possible in narrow windows.
5. If crypto moved, save transaction IDs and wallet addresses. File at ic3.gov with all evidence.
6. Change passwords on email, bank, and any account they saw on your video. Pull 2FA off SMS onto an authenticator app.
7. Lock your credit at Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion.
8. File at reportfraud.ftc.gov.

## Red flags you might have missed (for next time)

- "Digital arrest" or "digital custody" is not a real legal concept anywhere in the US.
- Told you to stay on video and not leave your home or speak to family.
- Showed you a fake warrant, badge, or arrest document over video.
- Demanded that all your bank balances be "audited" by transfer.
- Escalated to a senior "judge" or "officer" to increase pressure.
