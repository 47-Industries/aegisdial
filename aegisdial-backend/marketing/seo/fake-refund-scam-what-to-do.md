# Fake Amazon / Apple refund scam — they got into your bank

A fake Amazon or Apple refund scam that ends with you on the verge of wiring money back for an "overpayment" is a scam with remote access as the weapon. If they're still on your screen, disconnect now and keep reading.

## What happens in this scam

You get an email or call about a renewal charge — Geek Squad, Norton, McAfee, Amazon Prime, Apple, PayPal — for something you didn't buy, with a number to call for a refund. A "refund agent" has you install remote-access software and log into your bank to process it. Then they claim they "accidentally" refunded $5,000 instead of $500 and beg you to wire, Zelle, or gift-card the extra back. No refund ever happened — they just moved money between your own accounts to make it look that way.

## Why it worked on you (it's not your fault)

This scam runs on reciprocity and pity: they "made a mistake" and you feel obligated to help. That's a completely different decision frame than "hand money to a stranger." Professional call-center crews run this script all day, all over the world, because it routinely works on kind, careful people. The script is the problem, not you.

## What to do in the next 15 minutes

1. Disconnect your computer from Wi-Fi. Unplug any ethernet. Right now.
2. Do not send any money. The "overpayment" is your own money moved between your own accounts.
3. Uninstall AnyDesk, TeamViewer, UltraViewer, LogMeIn, or any remote-access app they had you install.
4. From a different trusted device, change your email password and then your bank password. Pull 2FA off SMS onto an authenticator app.
5. Call your bank on the number printed on the back of your card. Tell them you gave someone remote access and which windows they had open.
6. Review recent transfers and Zelle activity. Any unauthorized transaction from the last hour may still be stoppable.
7. File at reportfraud.ftc.gov and at ic3.gov.
8. If they saw your SSN on screen, lock your credit at all three bureaus.

## Red flags you might have missed (for next time)

- Received a refund receipt for a subscription you never bought.
- The "agent" needed remote access to your computer to process a refund.
- A refund was supposedly "overpaid" and you need to send back the difference.
- Asked you to Zelle, wire, or buy gift cards to "return" the extra.
- Cried, begged, or said they'd lose their job if you didn't help immediately.
