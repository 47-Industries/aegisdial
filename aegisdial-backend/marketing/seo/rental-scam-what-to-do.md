# Rental scam — you sent deposit to a fake listing

If a rental scam just took your deposit for a listing you can't actually move into, the next hour is where some of that money has a chance of coming back. Wire recalls close fast. Here's what to do.

## What happens in this scam

You find a too-good-to-be-true listing on Craigslist, Zillow, or Facebook Marketplace — often a real property scraped from a real agent's listing with a new "owner" email. The "owner" claims to be overseas and can't show the property but will mail keys after you wire a deposit and first month's rent. You send the money and either get nothing back or show up to a property that isn't for rent.

## Why it worked on you (it's not your fault)

Tight housing markets make "act fast" pressure feel genuine. Photos of a real property (stolen from the real listing) defeat the usual image-check people do. The scammers know what a competitive rental market feels like and shape the story to match it. This happens to first-time renters, grad students, relocating families, and people with excellent judgment every day.

## What to do in the next 15 minutes

1. Call your bank on the number from the back of your card right now. Tell them you wired money to a rental scammer and ask about a recall. Wires have the narrowest window — sometimes hours — so speed matters.
2. If you used Zelle, ask the bank specifically about the Zelle dispute process.
3. If you used a credit card or Cash App, dispute the charge with the issuer or app today.
4. Save every screenshot: the original listing, the email thread, payment receipts, the "owner's" email and phone.
5. Verify the real owner through public property records (most counties have a free tax assessor lookup) and, if you want, contact the real listing agent on the original site so they can report the spoof.
6. File at reportfraud.ftc.gov and at ic3.gov.
7. Also report the listing to the platform (Craigslist, Zillow, Facebook Marketplace) so it's taken down for the next person.

## Red flags you might have missed (for next time)

- Owner "out of the country" and can't show the unit.
- Asked for wire, Zelle, or crypto for deposit — not a normal rental payment.
- Rent far below comparable units in the neighborhood.
- Pressured to pay before a walk-through or lease signing.
- Listing photos matched a real for-sale or rented-elsewhere property (reverse image search).
