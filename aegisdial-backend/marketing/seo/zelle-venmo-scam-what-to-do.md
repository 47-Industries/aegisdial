# Zelle or Venmo scam — recover your money

A Zelle or Venmo scam usually hits in two waves: the first scam, and then a fake "support" callback that double-taxes you while you're still rattled. If you're in either wave right now, the next 15 minutes are where recovery lives.

## What happens in this scam

After you reported the original scam inside the Zelle, Venmo, or Cash App, someone calls claiming to be platform "support" — or you Googled a support number and got a fake one. They walk you through a "refund verification" that requires sending money back to yourself, but the steps route it to the scammer's account. Sometimes they repeat the "test" with larger amounts and drain the account further.

## Why it worked on you (it's not your fault)

You were already rattled from the first scam. "Support" calling you felt like help, not a second attack. That's exactly the psychological window the second-wave scam is built to exploit. It works on people who had just done the right thing — reporting inside the app — not on careless people.

## What to do in the next 15 minutes

1. Stop sending anything to anyone, even to "yourself." Your app does not require test sends.
2. Close the app and reopen it. Contact support only through the in-app help center of Zelle, Venmo, or Cash App — never through a Google-searched number.
3. For Zelle transfers, call your bank on the number on the back of your card. Under consumer protection rules, unauthorized Zelle transfers may be reimbursable; authorized-but-induced is trickier but still worth a case.
4. For Venmo or Cash App, file in-app and request a dispute. Screenshot every message.
5. Change your app password and your bank password from a trusted device.
6. Pull 2FA off SMS onto an authenticator app.
7. File at reportfraud.ftc.gov and, if a large amount moved, at ic3.gov.
8. Tell a family member — scam lists recirculate, and you will get another call.

## Red flags you might have missed (for next time)

- Unsolicited callback from "Zelle / Venmo / Cash App support" after you reported a scam.
- Instructions to send money to yourself — these apps don't require that.
- A "senior support" tier that asks you to install remote-access software.
- Pressure to act before the transaction "times out."
- Phone number you got from a Google search ad, not the platform's in-app contact.
