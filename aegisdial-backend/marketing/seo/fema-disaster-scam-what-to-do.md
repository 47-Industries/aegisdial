# FEMA disaster relief fraud — hurricane scam

FEMA disaster relief fraud hits neighborhoods that have already been hit — hurricane, wildfire, flood, tornado — and targets people already exhausted. If someone charged you a fee to "fast-track" FEMA aid, that is not how FEMA works. Let's undo the damage.

## What happens in this scam

After a federally declared disaster, a "FEMA inspector" or "relief agent" calls or visits offering to fast-track your application for a fee. They ask for your SSN, bank info, and disaster case number "to expedite the grant." They either steal the identity info, collect the fee, or submit a fraudulent application in your name and redirect the payout.

## Why it worked on you (it's not your fault)

Disaster victims are exhausted and confused about the aid process. A helper-figure with official-sounding language is hard to refuse when you've just lost a roof or a home. Scammers follow disaster declarations with precision and run this script on every affected zip code. You were not naive. You were hit twice.

## What to do in the next 15 minutes

1. Apply for real FEMA assistance only at disasterassistance.gov or the official FEMA helpline. Your real case is handled there — not through any agent who approached you.
2. If you paid a "fast-track" fee by card, dispute it with your card issuer today as services not as described. If you paid by cash or check, file a police report — both will help downstream.
3. If you gave your SSN and bank info, go to identitytheft.gov and start the recovery workflow. It's free.
4. Place a fraud alert and credit freeze at Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion.
5. Call your bank on the number from your card to flag recent activity and watch for unauthorized deposits or withdrawals.
6. Report suspected fraud to the FEMA disaster fraud hotline and to the DOJ's National Center for Disaster Fraud.
7. File at reportfraud.ftc.gov.
8. Warn your neighbors. Disaster fraud runs block by block.

## Red flags you might have missed (for next time)

- Any "FEMA" representative charging a fee — FEMA assistance is free.
- Asked for your SSN and bank info in the same in-person or phone interaction.
- Used generic badges without a visible federal agency ID.
- Showed up to high-damage neighborhoods door-to-door offering fast-track service.
- Pressured you to sign a contract with a specific contractor on the spot.
