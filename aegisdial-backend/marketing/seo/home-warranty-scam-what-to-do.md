# Home warranty scam call — what to do

A home warranty scam call usually lands within weeks of closing on a house, when public records expose your name and address. If you already paid an activation fee for something you're not sure you need, you still have options.

## What happens in this scam

A mailer or call says your "home warranty is about to expire" — usually right after you buy a house and your name hits public records. They pitch a home service plan that supposedly covers HVAC, appliances, and plumbing with a low monthly fee and a one-time activation payment. Either the company doesn't exist, denies every claim as "pre-existing," or charges a steep cancellation fee when you try to leave.

## Why it worked on you (it's not your fault)

New homeowners are overwhelmed and genuinely anxious about surprise repair bills. The official-looking mailer uses fake urgency and a real address pulled from public records. It sounds like an extension of what your lender, realtor, or builder set up — exactly the confusion the scammers designed. Millions of these mailers go out every year, and they look more convincing every cycle.

## What to do in the next 15 minutes

1. Stop any automatic renewal. Cancel autopay on the card or bank account they're billing.
2. If you paid by credit card in the last 60 days, dispute the charge with your card issuer as "services not rendered as described." Federal Regulation Z gives you dispute rights.
3. If you paid by debit or ACH, call your bank on the number from your card — disputes are possible but often narrower.
4. Pull the company's state license status at your state attorney general's consumer protection site or your state insurance department (some home warranty companies are regulated as insurers).
5. Research home warranty companies at your state consumer protection office or the BBB before signing any new policy.
6. File a complaint with your state attorney general and the BBB.
7. File at reportfraud.ftc.gov.
8. If you were pressured in person by a door-knocker, save the business card, brochure, and any signed paperwork. Most states give a three-day right to cancel door-to-door sales.

## Red flags you might have missed (for next time)

- Mailer or call within weeks of closing on a home, addressed to the exact owner name.
- Claimed to be affiliated with your builder, realtor, or lender without proof.
- Demanded an activation fee up front.
- No written contract before payment.
- Exclusions in the fine print that make nearly every claim "pre-existing."
