# Bitcoin ATM scam — what to do after sending crypto

If a Bitcoin ATM scam just had you feeding cash into a kiosk at a gas station or convenience store, you may have a very short window to act. Some kiosk operators can halt transfers that are still pending. Move now.

## What happens in this scam

A caller impersonating the IRS, SSA, a bank, or a sheriff tells you your money is at risk and must be "deposited to a secure federal wallet" through a Bitcoin kiosk. They keep you on the phone while you drive to a specific location. At the kiosk, they text you a QR code wallet address; you feed cash in, scan their code, and the coins route directly to them in seconds.

## Why it worked on you (it's not your fault)

The caller framed the kiosk as a government tool — sometimes calling it a "federal treasury machine" or a "safe deposit kiosk." Staying on the phone prevents any store employee or bystander from breaking the spell. That isolation tactic is used because it works on sharp, careful people under fear of arrest.

## What to do in the next 15 minutes

1. If you're still at the kiosk, walk away from the phone. Hang up. Take your receipt if one printed.
2. Save the paper receipt — it has a transaction ID. This is the single most important piece of evidence.
3. Call the kiosk operator listed on the machine (CoinFlip, Bitcoin Depot, CoinCloud, etc.) and tell them the transaction was coerced by a scam. A small number of transfers can be halted if still pending.
4. Call your local police non-emergency line. Some jurisdictions have fraud units specifically covering kiosk coercion.
5. File at ic3.gov with the transaction ID, wallet address, and kiosk location. This is the filing the FBI can actually work.
6. File at reportfraud.ftc.gov.
7. If you withdrew cash from a bank today, tell the bank what happened — they often have additional fraud support.
8. Tell one family member. Scammers often call back.

## Red flags you might have missed (for next time)

- Any instruction to deposit cash into a Bitcoin ATM to "protect" or "verify" your money.
- A QR code texted to you to scan at the kiosk.
- Told to tell the store clerk the money is for "family" or a "car purchase" if asked.
- Told to stay on the phone while driving and during the deposit.
- Agency, bank, or officer instructing the trip — none use crypto kiosks.
