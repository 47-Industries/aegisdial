# Utility shutoff scam — threatening call from fake power company

If a utility shutoff scam just had you standing in a checkout aisle with prepaid cards in your hand, stop. Real US utilities do not take payment that way. Your power, gas, and water are not actually at risk.

## What happens in this scam

A caller claims your power, gas, or water account is past due and a truck is dispatching to cut service within 30 to 60 minutes. They offer to stop the shutoff if you pay now on a prepaid card, gift card, or through a "secure utility portal" link they text you. They collect the payment codes or card info, and your service was never actually at risk.

## Why it worked on you (it's not your fault)

Fear of losing heat, AC, or lights in front of family or employees pushes even careful people into a 15-minute emergency decision. Add a spoofed caller ID showing the utility's real name, and the skepticism shelf is knocked out. This isn't carelessness. It's an emergency response pattern exploited on purpose.

## What to do in the next 15 minutes

1. Hang up. Do not return to any payment link they texted you.
2. Call your utility using the number on your last paper bill or on their official website. Ask about your actual account balance. Real utilities send written shutoff notices days or weeks in advance, not phone calls with countdowns.
3. If you already bought prepaid or gift cards and haven't redeemed them, call the card issuer's fraud line on the packaging immediately — some balances can be frozen.
4. If you entered card info at the fake "secure utility portal," call your bank on the number from your card and request a new card number plus a fraud review of the last hour.
5. Save screenshots of the text, any link, and any payment receipt. Screenshots help your bank and the utility's fraud team.
6. File at reportfraud.ftc.gov. Also report to your state's utility commission consumer complaint line — they often publish scam bulletins.
7. Tell a neighbor or family member. Utility shutoff scams run in waves through neighborhoods.

## Red flags you might have missed (for next time)

- Demanded payment by gift card or prepaid card — no US utility accepts these.
- Threatened disconnection within the hour with no prior written notice.
- Texted a payment link rather than using your utility's real app.
- Refused to let you hang up and call the utility back.
- Would not look up your account by service address, only by the caller's quoted number.
