# Charity scam — fake disaster donation call

A charity scam turns the best part of you — empathy — into the attack surface. If you donated on a call you didn't initiate, stop, breathe, and let's lock this down. The next 15 minutes matter.

## What happens in this scam

A call, text, email, or social post asks for donations to a cause you care about — often tied to a recent disaster, holiday drive, veterans' fund, police benevolent association, or a sick child. They push you to donate now by card, gift card, or crypto. The charity either doesn't exist, is registered but spends almost nothing on the cause, or is a direct impersonation of a real charity with a sound-alike name.

## Why it worked on you (it's not your fault)

Empathy plus timing (disaster, holiday) plus a sound-alike name defeats the skepticism people would apply to any other cold call. Scam PACs and scam charities spend real money on TV ads and call scripts to build legitimacy. This isn't a reflection on your judgment. It's a reflection on how sophisticated the fraud ecosystem has become.

## What to do in the next 15 minutes

1. Stop any recurring donation they may have set up. Check the card or bank statement for a recurring line item — scam charities commonly enable recurring donations by default.
2. If you donated by credit card, dispute the charge with your card issuer if you feel misled. Regulation Z gives you dispute rights on most card charges within 60 days.
3. If you donated by debit or ACH, call your bank on the number from your card and open a dispute.
4. If you donated by gift card, call each issuer's fraud line on the packaging — some balances can still be frozen.
5. If you shared SSN, address, or bank info, place a fraud alert and credit freeze at Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion.
6. Before giving again, verify the charity on Charity Navigator, GuideStar, or your state charity registry. Then donate directly on the charity's official website.
7. File at reportfraud.ftc.gov. Report the solicitation to your state attorney general and to the BBB Wise Giving Alliance.
8. Tell a family member. Your number is now on a "donor list" and more calls are coming.

## Red flags you might have missed (for next time)

- Name very similar to a well-known charity but not identical.
- Refused to provide EIN or state registration number.
- Pressured you to give today by phone rather than letting you look them up.
- Accepted gift cards or crypto for donations.
- Couldn't say what percentage of donations go to program vs. overhead.
