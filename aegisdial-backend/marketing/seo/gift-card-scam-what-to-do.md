# Gift card scam — what to do after you gave them the numbers

A gift card scam moves fast, but so can you. If the codes haven't been redeemed yet, some issuers can freeze the balance — and that's a small, real window the next 15 minutes are for.

## What happens in this scam

The caller — IRS, utility, "Amazon fraud," a grandchild's lawyer, whoever — creates a crisis and says the only fast fix is payment by gift card. They instruct you to drive to Target, Walmart, Walgreens, or a grocery store and buy specific brands (Apple, Google Play, Amazon, Target, eBay) in specific denominations, splitting the purchase across stores to avoid cashier questions. You read them the activation codes off the back. Within minutes, the value is drained overseas.

## Why it worked on you (it's not your fault)

Gift cards feel mundane and anonymous. They don't trigger the same hesitation as wiring money, and most cashiers have no scripted way to stop a customer who insists. The scammers rehearse scripts that tell you exactly what to say at the register. That was the script working — not you being gullible.

## What to do in the next 15 minutes

1. Stop buying cards. If the caller is still on the line, hang up.
2. If any cards are still in your hand and unread, call each issuer's gift-card fraud line printed on the card's packaging or their official website:
   - Apple: Apple gift card support
   - Google Play: Google Play fraud form
   - Amazon: Amazon gift card fraud
   - Target / Walmart / eBay: each has a published fraud page
3. Save every receipt. Gift card receipts are the evidence that makes issuer escalation possible.
4. Call your local police non-emergency line and file a report. Police reports help some issuers escalate a freeze.
5. File at reportfraud.ftc.gov.
6. If the caller was impersonating a specific agency (IRS, SSA, utility), also file with that agency's official fraud line.
7. Tell a family member. Scammers put you on a "paid" list. A second call is coming.

## Red flags you might have missed (for next time)

- Any agency or company taking payment by Apple, Google Play, Amazon, Target, or Walmart gift cards.
- Instructed to split purchases across multiple stores.
- Told what to say to the cashier if they ask.
- Told to read card numbers or photograph the back of the cards.
- Stayed on the phone with you during the drive and the purchase.
