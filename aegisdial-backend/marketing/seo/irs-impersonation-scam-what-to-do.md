# IRS scam call — what to do if you paid them

An IRS scam call that ended in payment is a bad moment, not a character flaw. If you paid in gift cards, wire, crypto, or at a Bitcoin ATM, there are still things to do in the next hour that matter — and you should do them before you do anything else.

## What happens in this scam

A caller says you owe back taxes and a warrant has already been issued or is about to be, usually reading off a fake badge number and case ID. They threaten arrest, deportation, or a suspended driver's license within the hour unless you pay right now. The payment method is always something a normal agency never uses — gift cards, a wire, cryptocurrency, or a Bitcoin ATM — "to settle before the warrant executes."

## Why it worked on you (it's not your fault)

Authority plus fear of jail plus a ticking clock is the single most effective script in the fraud playbook. Most adults have never been contacted by the IRS and don't know what normal looks like, so the countdown overrides judgment. Careful, intelligent people pay this scam every day. The script is designed against human nervous systems, not against stupid people.

## What to do in the next 15 minutes

1. Hang up if they're still on the line. Do not call any number they gave you.
2. If you paid by gift card and the codes haven't been redeemed yet, call the issuer's fraud line on the card's packaging — sometimes balances can be frozen.
3. If you paid by wire or Zelle, call your bank on the number from the back of your card and ask about a recall. Timing is everything here.
4. If you paid at a Bitcoin ATM, save the paper receipt (it has a transaction ID) and call the kiosk operator — a small minority of transfers can be halted if still pending.
5. File at reportfraud.ftc.gov and at ic3.gov.
6. If you shared your SSN, place a fraud alert at all three credit bureaus and freeze your credit. Consider an IRS IP PIN at the official IRS site to block future fraudulent returns.
7. Tell one family member. Scammers revisit people who paid — isolation is how they come back.

## Red flags you might have missed (for next time)

- Demanded payment by gift card, wire, crypto, or Bitcoin ATM — the real IRS never asks for these.
- Threatened immediate arrest, deportation, or license suspension.
- Refused to let you hang up and call the IRS back.
- Did not send any prior letter. The IRS contacts taxpayers by mail first.
- Asked for your SSN to "verify" rather than your tax transcript PIN.
