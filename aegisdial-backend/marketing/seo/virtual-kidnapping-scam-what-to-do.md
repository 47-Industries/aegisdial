# Virtual kidnapping scam — call saying family member was taken

A virtual kidnapping scam is designed to turn off your ability to think. If a call just told you your child or spouse has been taken, the single most important thing is to verify your loved one is safe — and you can do that right now, even while they're still on the line.

## What happens in this scam

The caller screams that your child or spouse has been kidnapped — sometimes with a crying voice in the background, which may be real, cloned, or a stock clip. They demand you stay on the line, not contact anyone, and drive to a Western Union, MoneyGram, or Bitcoin ATM for ransom. While you're isolated on the call, they extract as much cash as you can send before you discover your loved one is actually safe.

## Why it worked on you (it's not your fault)

The adrenaline of imagined child harm is the strongest psychological lever a scammer can pull. Being told "don't hang up" is an isolation tactic lifted from real kidnapping negotiations — and weaponized against loving parents and spouses. This is the most effective social-engineering script ever written, and it works on careful people. Every time.

## What to do in the next 15 minutes

1. Have someone else in the room, or another phone, call the supposed victim directly right now. Do not hang up the main call if that buys time.
2. Do not send money until you confirm.
3. If you already wired cash or sent a Bitcoin ATM deposit, call your bank on the number from your card for a recall, and the kiosk operator if it was crypto. Save any receipts and transaction IDs.
4. Call 911 or your local police non-emergency line once you're certain your loved one is safe.
5. File at reportfraud.ftc.gov and at ic3.gov if money moved.
6. Talk to your family today about agreeing on a safe word — one word only your family knows that a scammer cannot guess.
7. Tell the person whose name was used. They'll want to know their photo or social media may have been scraped, because more calls often follow.

## Red flags you might have missed (for next time)

- Caller said "don't hang up or we'll hurt them."
- Would not let you speak to your loved one directly.
- Demanded wire, gift card, or Bitcoin ATM — not a normal ransom medium.
- Call came while the supposed victim was at school, work, or somewhere you could verify.
- Crying in the background sounded looped or the same phrase repeated.
