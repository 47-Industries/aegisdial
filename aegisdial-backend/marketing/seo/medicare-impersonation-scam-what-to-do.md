# Medicare scam call — what to do if you shared your number

If a Medicare scam call got your Medicare number or MBI, the damage is real but contained. Your MBI is a billing identifier, not a bank account — which means fast action now can stop most of the downstream harm.

## What happens in this scam

A caller says Medicare is issuing new cards — plastic, chip-enabled, DNA-eligible, or a "2026 upgrade" — and just needs to "verify" your Medicare number. They walk you through confirming your MBI, date of birth, and sometimes bank or supplement plan details. Those numbers then get used to bill Medicare for fraudulent services, sold on, or to enroll you in a plan you never asked for.

## Why it worked on you (it's not your fault)

Medicare is genuinely confusing, and the caller sounds like they're doing you a favor by helping you not miss out on a benefit. That "helping you" frame is the trick. Seasoned fraud rings run this script at scale across millions of numbers because it works on careful people who simply haven't seen the pattern before.

## What to do in the next 15 minutes

1. Hang up if they're still on.
2. Call 1-800-MEDICARE (the number on your Medicare card) and tell them your MBI was shared with a suspected scammer. They will flag your account.
3. Watch your Medicare Summary Notices (MSNs) over the next 90 days for claims you don't recognize. Report any you see by calling 1-800-MEDICARE.
4. If you shared your SSN, bank info, or supplement plan details, call your bank on the card's number and place a fraud alert at all three credit bureaus.
5. File at reportfraud.ftc.gov. Consider also reporting to the HHS Office of Inspector General at the official OIG hotline.
6. Tell a family member — the same list that got your number often gets resold for follow-up calls.
7. If you were enrolled in a plan you didn't agree to, you usually have disenrollment rights. Call 1-800-MEDICARE or your SHIP counselor.

## Red flags you might have missed (for next time)

- Asked for your Medicare number, MBI, or SSN to "issue a new card."
- Offered a free DNA test, back brace, or diabetic supplies in exchange for your MBI.
- Claimed Medicare is issuing new chip cards (there is no such program).
- Pressured you to answer today or "lose the benefit."
- Called you without you ever contacting Medicare first.
