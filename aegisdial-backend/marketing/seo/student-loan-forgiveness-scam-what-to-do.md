# Student loan forgiveness scam — they took your info

A student loan forgiveness scam is hard to spot because federal loan rules actually do change, and the callers sound like they're reading from a real memo. If a "forgiveness specialist" charged you or asked for your FSA ID, you need to lock things down today.

## What happens in this scam

A caller or ad promises to "forgive" your federal student loans, lower payments to $0, or get you into a special program "only available today." They charge an upfront fee of $400 to $1,500 or a monthly "service" fee, sometimes asking for your FSA ID credentials "to process it." Either they do nothing, sign you up for a free program you could have done yourself, or change your FSA password and redirect refunds.

## Why it worked on you (it's not your fault)

Federal student-loan rules change often and are genuinely confusing. "Limited-time" urgency exploits that confusion. Scammers rent data lists that identify federal loan holders and target them directly. This is a scam built for borrowers who were actually trying to do the right thing — exactly the people who don't deserve it.

## What to do in the next 15 minutes

1. Change your FSA ID password at the official studentaid.gov site right now. If you can't log in, use the account recovery flow immediately.
2. Revoke any power of attorney on file at your loan servicer — call the servicer directly using the number on studentaid.gov.
3. If you paid an upfront fee by credit card in the last 60 days, dispute it with your card issuer as services not as described.
4. If you paid by debit or ACH, call your bank on the number from your card to dispute.
5. Federal forgiveness and income-driven repayment plans are always free to apply for at studentaid.gov. Anything real you need, you can do yourself there.
6. File with the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau at consumerfinance.gov/complaint, the FTC at reportfraud.ftc.gov, and your state attorney general.
7. Watch your loan servicer account for unauthorized refund redirects — scammers sometimes change the deposit account.
8. Tell one family member. Scam lists resell — follow-up "final approval" calls often come next.

## Red flags you might have missed (for next time)

- Any company charging an upfront fee to forgive federal loans — federal forgiveness is free to apply for.
- Asked for your FSA ID (login for studentaid.gov).
- Claimed access to a "secret" Biden / Trump / Department of Education program.
- Told you must act "before the window closes."
- Took a power-of-attorney that let them collect your refunds.
