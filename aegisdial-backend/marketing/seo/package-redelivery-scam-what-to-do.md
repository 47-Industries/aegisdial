# USPS / FedEx / UPS redelivery text scam — what to do

A USPS, FedEx, or UPS redelivery text scam lands because almost everyone has a package moving at any given moment. If you tapped the link and entered your card info, the window to kill the damage is measured in minutes, not hours.

## What happens in this scam

A text from "USPS," "UPS," "FedEx," or "DHL" claims a package couldn't be delivered because of an address issue or a small customs/redelivery fee. The link takes you to a clone of the carrier's tracking page where you enter your address and card info for a small fee. The card is then used immediately for test charges and then larger ones, and the address info is added to a profile for future scams.

## Why it worked on you (it's not your fault)

Most people have a package in transit at any given moment, which makes the text feel personalized when it isn't. The domain looks close enough at a glance, and the fee is small enough that disputing it feels more expensive than paying it. Smishing texts are industrialized — crews send millions per day — and a small percentage is a lot of money.

## What to do in the next 15 minutes

1. Don't tap the link again. If you already entered card info, call your card issuer on the number printed on the back of your card. Request a new card number and a fraud review of the last hour.
2. If you used a debit card, this is especially urgent — debit fraud can drain a real bank balance. Ask the bank to freeze the account if needed.
3. If you used Apple Pay or Google Pay, remove the compromised card from the wallet on your phone.
4. Check your card activity online or in the bank's app for any unknown charge, even for $1 — test charges precede big ones.
5. If you were expecting a real package, go to the carrier's real app or official site (usps.com, fedex.com, ups.com) and enter the tracking number there. If there's a real issue, it will be shown.
6. Forward the scam text to 7726 (SPAM) on your phone. That's the industry reporting line — carriers use it.
7. Report USPS smishing to the US Postal Inspection Service at their official reporting page.
8. File at reportfraud.ftc.gov.

## Red flags you might have missed (for next time)

- USPS, UPS, or FedEx never text about a redelivery fee for a domestic package.
- Link domain didn't match the carrier's real domain.
- Small urgent fee asked over text.
- Text came from an email address or an unusual phone number.
- Page asked for full card info rather than routing to the carrier's real app.
