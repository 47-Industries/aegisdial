# Employment scam — they sent a fake check

If an employment scam sent you a check to deposit and forward — "for equipment" or "for a vendor" — stop before the forward. The bank will claw the check back in days and leave you on the hook for the full amount.

## What happens in this scam

A recruiter messages you on LinkedIn, Indeed, or by text about a remote job — data entry, package reshipping, payment processing, personal assistant, or mystery shopping. "Onboarding" asks for your SSN, driver's license, and bank info "for payroll." Sometimes a check arrives that you're told to deposit and forward part of "to a vendor" or "for equipment." The check bounces days later, or your account becomes a money mule moving stolen funds.

## Why it worked on you (it's not your fault)

Job-seekers are motivated and often under financial pressure. LinkedIn and a real company name (usually spoofed) bypass early skepticism. This is a playbook that's been running for a decade against sharp, employed professionals who wanted a side income. It's not a reflection on you.

## What to do in the next 15 minutes

1. Stop all communication with the "recruiter" — text, WhatsApp, Telegram, Gmail, all of it.
2. Do not deposit any check they sent you. If you already deposited and the funds "cleared," the funds will reverse in days. Do not forward anything.
3. Call your bank on the number from your card. Tell them what happened. They can place a hold on the deposit and advise on next steps.
4. If you reshipped packages from addresses you didn't buy from, stop and contact the US Postal Inspection Service (USPIS) at their official reporting page.
5. If you gave SSN or driver's license, go to identitytheft.gov and start the identity recovery plan. It's free.
6. Place a fraud alert and credit freeze at Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion.
7. File at reportfraud.ftc.gov. If large amounts or mule activity occurred, also file at ic3.gov.
8. Save every email, text, and document. Evidence is what helps your bank defend you.

## Red flags you might have missed (for next time)

- Hired without a real video interview or HR paperwork.
- Asked for SSN, license, and bank info before any signed offer.
- Sent you a check to deposit and "forward" to a vendor.
- Asked you to reship packages from addresses you didn't buy from.
- Communicated only over text, WhatsApp, Telegram, or personal Gmail.
