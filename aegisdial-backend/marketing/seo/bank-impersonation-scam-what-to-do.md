# Fake bank fraud call — what to do if you gave them access

If a fake bank fraud call just walked you into moving money, stop reading, breathe, and know the next hour decides more than the last one did. You are one of thousands of people this happens to every week, and the scam is engineered to beat calm, smart adults — not just careless ones.

## What happens in this scam

The caller ID shows your bank's real name and the voice on the line warns you about a suspicious wire or charge from another state. They walk you through fake "verification," then tell you a fraudster has inside access, so the only safe move is to transfer your balance to a "secure holding account." You get pushed toward Zelle, a wire, cash, or a Bitcoin ATM — and kept on the line the whole time so you can't call anyone to sanity-check.

## Why it worked on you (it's not your fault)

Professional scammers combine authority with urgency. The caller names transactions that feel plausible, uses real bank jargon, and flips the frame so you're their teammate fighting a fraudster instead of their target. That frame is built to bypass skepticism in a careful person under pressure. It is not stupidity. It is a playbook refined on millions of calls.

## What to do in the next 15 minutes

1. Hang up. Do not call any number the caller gave you.
2. Call your bank using the number printed on the back of your debit or credit card. Tell them you were walked into a transfer by someone impersonating the bank.
3. If money hasn't settled yet, ask the bank's real fraud team to stop or recall the pending transaction. Wires, Zelle, and ACH each have different windows — minutes matter.
4. Change your online banking password and email password from a device you trust. Pull 2FA off SMS and onto an authenticator app.
5. File a report at reportfraud.ftc.gov (FTC) and at ic3.gov (FBI) if any funds moved.
6. Lock your credit at Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion — free freezes at each bureau's site.
7. Tell one family member or close friend. Isolation is how this scam works; breaking it protects you from follow-ups.

## Red flags you might have missed (for next time)

- Asked you to move money to a "safe" or "holding" account — real banks never do this.
- Told you not to tell the teller or branch staff the real reason for the withdrawal.
- Stayed on the line while you drove to the bank or ATM.
- Read back real account details (from a data breach) to sound legitimate.
- Pushed wire, Zelle, cash, or crypto instead of the bank's normal dispute process.
