# Fake unpaid toll text scam (EZDriveMA, SunPass, FasTrak, etc.)

A fake unpaid toll text scam is cheap, fast, and convincing — a small dollar amount and a plausible agency name. If you tapped the link and entered card info, stop and lock it down now.

## What happens in this scam

A text from "E-ZPass," "SunPass," "FasTrak," "EZDriveMA," or your state's toll authority claims an unpaid toll of a small amount ($4 to $14) and threatens late fees or license suspension if not paid within 24 hours. The link takes you to a cloned agency page that collects your card details and sometimes driver's license info. The card is drained through rapid test purchases while the identity info is resold.

## Why it worked on you (it's not your fault)

Small amount plus plausible authority plus a short window is a textbook phishing combo. It's cheaper to pay $12 than to argue — which is exactly what the scammers count on. Every licensed driver gets these texts. Falling for one says nothing about you except that you have a phone.

## What to do in the next 15 minutes

1. Don't tap the link. If you already entered card info, call your card issuer on the number printed on the back of your card. Request a new card number.
2. If you used a debit card, speed matters more — call your bank and ask them to freeze the card and review the last hour of activity.
3. Check your card activity online for any unknown charge, even for $1. Small test charges precede the drain.
4. If you also entered your driver's license or address info, watch for secondary identity scams. Consider a credit freeze at Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion.
5. To check if you actually owe a real toll, go directly to your toll agency's official app or website (search the agency name plus your state). Never through a text link.
6. Forward the scam text to 7726 (SPAM) on your phone.
7. Report the text to your state toll authority — many publish a dedicated scam reporting email.
8. File at reportfraud.ftc.gov and, if the dollar amount taken was large, at ic3.gov.

## Red flags you might have missed (for next time)

- Link domain didn't match your actual toll agency's real domain.
- Text threatened license suspension over a $10 toll.
- Demanded payment within hours.
- Text came from an email or overseas number.
- Wouldn't accept payment through the toll agency's real app.
